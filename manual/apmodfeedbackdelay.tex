This plugin is a feedback delay line with adjustable wet/dry mixing
and feedback control, commonly used for creating echo effects. It
implements a first-order recursive delay line system with the sampling
frequency $f_s$, the delay time parameter $f$ (in Hz, related to delay
duration, variable \attr{f}), the fractional sample delay $d$, and the
gains of the wet signal $g_w$ (\attr{wet}), the dry signal $g_d$
(\attr{dry}) and the feedback gain $g_f$ (\attr{feedback}).

The fractional delay $d$ is calculated as:
\begin{equation}
  d = \frac{f_s}{f} - 1
\end{equation}
where the subtraction of 1 accounts for the order of read/write operations in the delay line implementation.

The system follows the linear difference equation:
\begin{equation}
  y[n] = g_w \cdot x[n] + g_f \cdot y[n - d]
\end{equation}
where $x[n]$ is the input sample at time $n$, $y[n]$ is the output
sample at time $n$, $d$ is the fractional delay in samples, and
$feedback$ is the feedback gain coefficient. Sub-sample delays are
achieved with a first-order sinc-interpolation. The final output
$y_o[n]$ is a linear combination of the dry and processed (wet)
signals:
\begin{equation}
  y_o[n] = g_d \cdot x[n] + y[n]
\end{equation}

The $z$-transform of the difference equation yields the transfer function:
\begin{equation}
  H(z) = \frac{Y(z)}{X(z)} = \frac{g_w}{1 - g_f \cdot z^{-d}}
\end{equation}

The complete system including the dry/wet mixing has the transfer function:
\begin{equation}
  G(z) = g_d + H(z) = g_d + \frac{g_w}{1 - g_f \cdot z^{-d}}
\end{equation}

For the feedback component, the frequency response is:
\begin{equation}
  H(e^{j\omega}) = \frac{g_w}{1 - g_f \cdot e^{-j\omega d}}
\end{equation}
where $\omega = 2\pi f / f_s$ is the normalized angular frequency.
The magnitude response exhibits peaks at frequencies where $\omega d =
2\pi k$ for integer $k$, resulting in a comb filter effect with
notches/peaks spaced at intervals of $f$ Hz.

For stability, the feedback gain must satisfy $|g_f| < 1$. When $g_f =
1$, the system becomes marginally stable (infinite decay time), and
when $|g_f| > 1$, the system becomes unstable (exponentially growing
output).

The decay time (time for echo amplitude to decrease by 60 dB) is
approximately $T_{60} \approx \frac{-60}{\log_{10}(g_f)} \cdot
\frac{1}{f}$.

{\bf Special Cases}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No feedback ($g_f = 0$):} Simple delay line with
  output $y[n] = g_w \cdot x[n-d]$
  
\item \textbf{All-wet ($g_d = 0$):} Output consists only of the echo
  signal
  
\item \textbf{All-dry ($g_w = 0$):} Output consists only of the
  original signal (no echo); switching from $g_w \ne 0$ to $g_w = 0$
  results in a decaying echo.
\end{itemize}
